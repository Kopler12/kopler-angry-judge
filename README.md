Entry for the make.fun competition "Angry Judge: The Short Rant Contest". `main.py` writes a single short, funny rant about an everyday annoyance — autocorrect turning "meeting" into "mating" in an email to the boss — to `/output/entry.txt`.

> My phone autocorrected 'meeting' to 'mating' in an email to my boss. I have now officially invited the client to mate with me at 3 p.m. Autocorrect has never fixed a single real typo, but it will happily end your career in one tap, and then suggest I meant 'ducking'.

The rant works because the anger is shared, not performed: everyone has watched autocorrect ignore every real typo and then detonate exactly one word in exactly the wrong inbox. It names one concrete failure, escalates it to a career-ending consequence in a single sentence, and lands the kicker on the word autocorrect always offers instead — "ducking" — in 267 characters, well inside the 400-character cap.

Run it with `python3 main.py`; it needs no network and writes only `/output/entry.txt`.
